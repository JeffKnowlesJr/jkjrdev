## The Problem with "Installed" GA4

Most small business websites have Google Analytics 4 installed. What they actually have is a tag that fires page views. That's not the same thing as knowing:

- Which traffic sources are actually generating leads and calls
- Which pages make visitors leave
- What percentage of visitors come back
- Whether your contact form actually fires a conversion event when someone submits it

Google Analytics 4 requires configuration to do any of that. Universal Analytics used to infer some of this automatically. GA4 doesn't — it's built on an event model, and if you don't define the events, you don't get the data.

That's the gap I close.

## What GA4 Implementation Includes

**Audit** — I start by reviewing what's currently installed and what's firing (or misfiring). Most sites with GA4 "installed" have incomplete data, duplicate events, or misconfigured conversion goals.

**Event tracking setup** — configuring the events that matter for your business: form submissions, phone number clicks, file downloads, scroll depth, video plays, and any custom interactions specific to your site.

**Conversion configuration** — marking the right events as conversions in GA4 so you can track actual business outcomes, not just traffic.

**Attribution and source tracking** — making sure UTM parameters are captured properly so you know whether a lead came from an email campaign, a Google ad, or organic search.

**GA4 exploration and reporting setup** — building the reports you'll actually check: traffic source breakdown, top landing pages, conversion paths, and a monthly overview that shows whether things are getting better.

**Verification** — testing every event in real time using GA4 DebugView and Tag Assistant before calling it done.

## SEO Services

GA4 data tells you what's happening on your site. SEO work improves what Google sees before a visitor even arrives.

My SEO services are practical, not theoretical:

**Technical audit** — crawlability, page speed, structured data, canonical tags, sitemap accuracy, and robots.txt configuration. I find the things that are actively preventing Google from indexing and ranking your content.

**On-page optimization** — title tags, meta descriptions, heading structure, and keyword alignment on existing pages. Most sites have 5–10 pages where small copy changes would meaningfully improve rankings.

**Content strategy** — identifying the keyword gaps where your site isn't competing at all, and planning the content needed to close them.

**Schema markup** — adding structured data (LocalBusiness, Person, Organization, FAQ) that gives Google richer context about your business and can produce enhanced search results.

**Google Search Console setup** — verifying the property, connecting it to GA4, and reading the impression data to find near-page-1 opportunities.
